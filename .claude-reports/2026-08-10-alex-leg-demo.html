The pipeline runs on the real Alex model

Branch pr4-alex-demo puts a randomized marker set on Alex's legs and drives the whole chain — calibration, runtime, gates — on the URDF the Python estimator actually uses. Until now every accuracy claim was measured against a toy 6-DOF robot.

Source: invariant-estimation/assets/alex_with_imus.urdf, vendored byte-identically · 168 tests, 0 failures, 0 skipped

It works, and the real robot is better conditioned than the toy. The URDF loads unmodified — capsule collisions, 17 missing hand meshes, zero-mass IMU frames and all. The CoM computed from marker positions agrees with Mecano's independent calculator to 1.1e-15 m. Three findings below are worth acting on before hardware time.

What was measured

URDF load 29joints 30 links, 91.5126 kg, root PELVIS_LINK. No sanitizer needed.
CoM vs Mecano 1.1e-15m Two paths sharing no code. The loop closes.
Layout error 0.314mm σ = 0.3 mm, K = 30. Toy was 0.383 mm.
Pelvis attitude 0.437° The ground truth's own noise floor at a 140 mm bracket.

1 · The pelvis bracket has to be wider than the spec says

Held-out marker RMS scales as 1/r_perp in the gauge cluster's spread and linearly in mocap noise. On log–log axes that power law is a straight line, and all three noise levels are parallel — which is the law itself, drawn.

At FRAMEWORK §1's recommended 140 mm bracket and §17's 0.3 mm target noise, Alex lands at 2.86 mm against the 2.2 mm TALOS bar — on synthetic data with a perfect URDF, so this is a floor rather than an estimate.

Actionable

To clear 2.2 mm on Alex you need a bracket of at least ~182 mm at σ = 0.3 mm, or σ ≤ 0.23 mm at 140 mm. This is a bracket-design number you can act on before any hardware time.

2 · A leg-only marker set leaves most of the robot on encoders

Unmarked links get their pose by FK chaining from the nearest marked ancestor (F7) — which reintroduces exactly the encoder dependence F6 exists to remove. With pelvis and legs marked, 58.45 % of Alex's 91.5 kg has its pose from FK rather than markers.

TORSO_LINK alone is 22.21 kg — a quarter of the robot — chained through the single SPINE_Z joint, which is also one of the filter's nine states.

3 · A degenerate marker set looks perfect and is 57 mm wrong

PR2 predicted this from the joint axes alone: if every marked link merely rotates about a common axis, a translation along that axis is an exact symmetry of the objective. Pelvis plus both HIP_X_LINK is that case on Alex — both are R_x(q) with parallel axes. Planted deliberately, on noiseless data:

What the fit reports 0.112mm In-sample marker RMS. Exit code 0. G2 passes. isFullySolved() returns true.
How wrong it actually is 57.3mm Every marker displaced by the same 55.9 mm vector along x. The y and z components are 0.43 % of it.
The part that matters

σ₃ cannot detect this. The degenerate set reads 8.79e-4 m² against 2.21e-3 for an identifiable one — a factor of 2.5, while the gap between two both-identifiable sets was 26. Identifiability has to be checked from the joint axes when the marker set is designed; it is not recoverable from the data afterwards.

4 · The CoM is still as good as the URDF, not as good as the mocap

F11's budget on Alex's real inertial blocks. The two CAD-sourced terms outweigh the one term this pipeline controls by 33×, so perfect mocap would change the CoM by essentially nothing.

What to be wary of

  • SCS2 silently rewrites link frames. transformAllFramesToZUp() defaults to on, zeroing each joint's rotation and compensating in the axis and inertia. Physics preserved, frame identity not — so my PR2 javadoc claim that a calibrated ^i p̂_ij is "directly comparable to a CAD marker position" is false above the legs. True for the legs only because they all have rpy="0 0 0".
  • The 1.0 mm layout threshold is seed-sensitive — that statistic ranges 0.45–2.47 mm across 10 seeds, driven by the unaveraged reference-capture noise. A known mechanism, not a flake, but don't read one run as precise.
  • The TALOS-bar assertion has ~30 % margin after 3-seed averaging. One seed put it at 2.2004 mm against a 2.2000 mm bar; the agent refused to ship that and averaged instead.
  • G2 does not fire on a 0.5° hip offset at σ = 0.3 mm — floored by Alex's own gauge error — and when it does fire it lights both branches, not one. Weaker localisation than on the toy.

What I would do next

  1. Decide the §3 CAD-comparability wording. It is a spec claim now known false above the legs, and it was one of the two amendments already flagged on PR #2.
  2. Re-spec the pelvis bracket at ≥182 mm. Cheapest accuracy available, and now quantified rather than recommended.
  3. Add a torso cluster. 22.2 kg off encoders, and SPINE_Z is a filter state.
  4. Weigh the robot. Minutes, and it constrains the single largest term in the CoM budget.
